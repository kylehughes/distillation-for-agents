# Research sources

Use this register to locate the intellectual foundations of Distill. IDs R01–R53 retain the numbering of the source report. Prefer the indicated passage over an isolated aphorism. Consult the edition and caveat before quoting or extending a claim. These works inform the procedure; they do not supply current domain-specific requirements or establish that a proposed application is correct.

## Stoic and classical inquiry

### R01

**Marcus Aurelius. [Meditations Book 3](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.3.three.html).** Second century CE.

**Passage:** 3.11.

**Edition/source:** George Long translation.

**Use and caveat:** Description, composition, duration, relation to the whole, and the virtue required.

### R02

**Marcus Aurelius. [Meditations Book 8](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.8.eight.html).** Second century CE.

**Passage:** 8.11, 8.13, 8.49.

**Edition/source:** George Long translation.

**Use and caveat:** Constitution and activity; physical, ethical, and logical examination; reported fact and added judgment.

### R03

**Marcus Aurelius. [Meditations Book 6](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html).** Second century CE.

**Passage:** 6.13.

**Edition/source:** George Long translation.

**Use and caveat:** Physical redescription of prestigious objects.

### R04

**Marcus Aurelius. [Meditations Book 11](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.11.eleven.html).** Second century CE.

**Passage:** 11.2.

**Edition/source:** George Long translation.

**Use and caveat:** Decomposing music and movement to loosen attachment.

### R05

**Marcus Aurelius. [Meditations Book 4](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.4.four.html).** Second century CE.

**Passage:** 4.24.

**Edition/source:** George Long translation.

**Use and caveat:** Do what rational and social obligation requires; remove unnecessary action and thought.

### R06

**Epictetus. [Enchiridion](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html).** Second century CE.

**Passage:** Sections 1 and 5.

**Edition/source:** Elizabeth Carter translation.

**Use and caveat:** Discriminate what is up to us and examine impressions.

### R07

**Plato. [Meno](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/meno.html).** Fourth century BCE.

**Passage:** 71b–73c, especially 72a–73c.

**Edition/source:** Benjamin Jowett translation.

**Use and caveat:** Socrates asks for a defining account rather than a list of virtues.

### R08

**Plato. [Phaedrus](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/phaedrus.html).** Fourth century BCE.

**Passage:** 265d–266b.

**Edition/source:** Benjamin Jowett translation.

**Use and caveat:** Collection and division according to the subject's articulations.

### R09

**Aristotle. [Metaphysics Book 7](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.7.vii.html).** Fourth century BCE.

**Passage:** VII.4 and VII.10–11; compare VII.17.

**Edition/source:** W. D. Ross translation.

**Use and caveat:** Essence, accidental attributes, matter, and form; the online ending of VII.17 is incomplete.

### R10

**Aristotle. [Physics Book 2](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.2.ii.html).** Fourth century BCE.

**Passage:** II.3, 194b16–195a3; compare II.7.

**Edition/source:** R. P. Hardie and R. K. Gaye translation.

**Use and caveat:** Four senses of cause or explanation.

### R11

**Aristotle. [Nicomachean Ethics Book 1](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html).** Fourth century BCE.

**Passage:** I.3, 1094b11–27.

**Edition/source:** W. D. Ross translation.

**Use and caveat:** The degree of precision should fit the subject.

## Daoist and Buddhist analysis

### R12

**Laozi, traditional attribution. [Daodejing selections](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_china-laozi-daodejing.htm).** Ancient Chinese text.

**Passage:** Chapter 48.

**Edition/source:** Sources of Chinese Tradition, de Bary and Bloom, 1999; Columbia University selections.

**Use and caveat:** Diminishing in pursuit of the Way; read alongside the introduction on attribution.

### R13

**Laozi, traditional attribution. [Daodejing selections on government](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_china-laozi-govt.htm).** Ancient Chinese text.

**Passage:** Chapter 57.

**Edition/source:** Sources of Chinese Tradition, de Bary and Bloom, 1999; Columbia University.

**Use and caveat:** The possible counterproductive effects of proliferating intervention.

### R14

**Laozi, traditional attribution. [Daodejing Chapter 63](https://www.yellowbridge.com/onlinelit/daodejing63.php?characterMode=t).** Ancient Chinese text.

**Passage:** Chapter 63.

**Edition/source:** Parallel translations including James Legge, YellowBridge.

**Use and caveat:** Attend to difficult things while they remain small; translations differ.

### R15

**Zhuangzi. [Nourishing the Lord of Life](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/nourishing-the-lord-of-life).** Ancient Chinese text.

**Passage:** Inner Chapters, chapter 3, Cook Ding narrative.

**Edition/source:** James Legge translation, Chinese Text Project.

**Use and caveat:** Skill through structural responsiveness, with caution at difficult joints.

### R16

**Vajira in the early Buddhist canon. [Vajira Sutta](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn05/sn05.010.bodh.html).** Early Buddhist text.

**Passage:** Samyutta Nikaya 5.10.

**Edition/source:** Bhikkhu Bodhi translation.

**Use and caveat:** The chariot analogy is spoken by the nun Vajira.

### R17

**Early Buddhist canon. [Kaccayanagotta Sutta](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.015.than.html).** Early Buddhist text.

**Passage:** Samyutta Nikaya 12.15.

**Edition/source:** Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation.

**Use and caveat:** Conditional arising and cessation between the extremes of existence and nonexistence.

### R18

**Nagarjuna. [Mulamadhyamakakarika or Verses from the Centre](https://leighb.com/VfromC.htm).** Second to third century CE.

**Passage:** 15.1–2; 24.8–10 and 24.18–19.

**Edition/source:** Stephen Batchelor, literal translation from Tibetan, April 2000.

**Use and caveat:** Intrinsic nature, dependent arising, emptiness, and conventional designation.

## Explanation, inquiry, and communication

### R19

**William of Ockham, quoted and discussed by Elliott Sober. [Ockham's Razors](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781316371534_A25416545/preview-9781316371534_A25416545.pdf).** 2015.

**Passage:** Pages 11–12; Reportatio II, question 18, Opera Theologica V, p. 404.

**Edition/source:** Cambridge University Press; primary passage translated by Rega Wood.

**Use and caveat:** A primary passage transmitted within a modern scholarly study, not an independent edition of Ockham.

### R20

**Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. [William of Ockham](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2024/entries/ockham/).** Spring 2024 archive.

**Passage:** Discussion of parsimony.

**Edition/source:** Scholarly secondary reference.

**Use and caveat:** Useful for attribution and historical qualifications concerning the razor.

### R21

**Rene Descartes. [Discourse on the Method](https://web.viu.ca/johnstoi/descartes/descartes1.htm).** 1637.

**Passage:** Part II, four rules.

**Edition/source:** Ian Johnston translation, revised 2010, Vancouver Island University.

**Use and caveat:** Assent, decomposition, orderly reconstruction, and completeness.

### R22

**Francis Bacon. [Novum Organum](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45988/45988-h/45988-h.htm).** 1620.

**Passage:** Book I, aphorisms 39–46 and 59–60.

**Edition/source:** Joseph Devey edition, 1902.

**Use and caveat:** Projected order, confirmation, and the misleading effects of inherited words.

### R23

**Isaac Newton. [Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/newton-princ.asp).** 1729 English translation.

**Passage:** Book III, Rules I, II and IV.

**Edition/source:** Andrew Motte translation, Fordham excerpt.

**Use and caveat:** Rule IV belongs to the 1726 third edition, not the 1687 first edition.

### R24

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz. [Discourse on Metaphysics](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Discourse_on_Metaphysics).** 1686.

**Passage:** Sections 5–6.

**Edition/source:** English primary text at Wikisource.

**Use and caveat:** Economical hypotheses, rich consequences, and the possibility of fitting arbitrary points.

### R25

**Immanuel Kant. [Critique of Pure Reason](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/humftp/E-text/Kant/cpr.kemp-smith/19dia-ap.htm).** 1781 and 1787.

**Passage:** Appendix to the Transcendental Dialectic; Kemp Smith pp. 538–549.

**Edition/source:** Norman Kemp Smith translation, Chinese University of Hong Kong.

**Use and caveat:** Regulative unity balanced with specification and differences.

### R26

**Albert Einstein. [On the Method of Theoretical Physics](https://sites.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/teaching/Einstein_graduate/2590_Einstein_2015/pdfs/Einstein_Method_Physics.pdf).** 1933 lecture.

**Passage:** Ideas and Opinions, pp. 270–276, especially p. 272.

**Edition/source:** Later English translation reproduced in a University of Pittsburgh scan.

**Use and caveat:** Do not conflate the wording of different English translations.

### R27

**Charles Sanders Peirce. [How to Make Our Ideas Clear](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p119.html).** 1878.

**Passage:** Popular Science Monthly 12, pp. 286–302, sections II–III.

**Edition/source:** Primary essay transcription.

**Use and caveat:** Clarify a concept through conceivable practical consequences.

### R28

**Edmund Husserl. [Experience and Judgment](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/106990513/Maudsley_Reader_in_Phenomenogical_Psychiatry_final_author_copy.pdf).** 1939.

**Passage:** Sections 86–93, especially 87; excerpt PDF pages 40–54.

**Edition/source:** Primary excerpt in The Maudsley Reader in Phenomenological Psychiatry, author copy.

**Use and caveat:** Free variation and essential seeing; distinguish this philosophical project from empirical generalization.

### R29

**Ludwig Wittgenstein. [Philosophical Investigations](https://sites.evergreen.edu/politicalshakespeares/wp-content/uploads/sites/226/2015/12/Ludwig.Wittgenstein.-.family-resemblance-excerpt.pdf).** 1953.

**Passage:** Sections 60–71, especially 65–67.

**Edition/source:** G. E. M. Anscombe translation excerpt, Evergreen State College.

**Use and caveat:** Family resemblance and the limitations of seeking a common essence.

### R30

**William James. [The Sentiment of Rationality](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/26659/pg26659-images.html).** Collected in 1897.

**Passage:** Section I; 1912 impression pp. 63–68.

**Edition/source:** The Will to Believe and Other Essays in Popular Philosophy.

**Use and caveat:** The competing attractions of unification and distinctness.

### R31

**Henri Poincare. [Science and Hypothesis](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Poincare/Poincare_1905_10.html).** 1905 English edition.

**Passage:** Chapter IX, Hypotheses in Physics, especially pp. 145–150.

**Edition/source:** Primary text at Brock University's Mead Project.

**Use and caveat:** Simple causes can generate complexity; simple appearances can aggregate complex processes.

### R32

**Alfred North Whitehead. [The Concept of Nature](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Whitehead/Whitehead_1920/White1_07.html).** 1920.

**Passage:** Chapter VII, Objects, p. 163.

**Edition/source:** Primary text at Brock University's Mead Project.

**Use and caveat:** The verified source of Seek simplicity and distrust it.

### R33

**John Dewey. [How We Think](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/37423/pg37423-images.html).** 1910.

**Passage:** Chapter VI, pp. 68–78; chapter VII, pp. 85–90.

**Edition/source:** Project Gutenberg primary text.

**Use and caveat:** Problem definition, proposed solutions, consequences, and testing; observation and inference.

### R34

**George Polya. [How to Solve It](https://jwilson.coe.uga.edu/emt725/References/Polya_HowToSolveIt.pdf).** 1945; later editions.

**Passage:** Opening four-phase guide; Auxiliary Problems; Decomposing and Recombining; Variation of the Problem.

**Edition/source:** Book scan hosted by University of Georgia.

**Use and caveat:** Transform a problem, solve a useful relative, and return to the original conditions.

### R35

**Richard Feynman. [What Is Science](https://feynman.com/science/what-is-science/).** 1966 address; published 1969.

**Passage:** The Physics Teacher 7, pp. 313–320; toy dog and naming examples.

**Edition/source:** Primary address transcription.

**Use and caveat:** Explain the mechanism without relying on a newly learned label.

### R36

**Henry David Thoreau. [Letter to H G O Blake](https://www.walden.org/quotation-category/morals-and-values/simplicity/).** 27 March 1848.

**Passage:** Passage on simplifying the problem of life.

**Edition/source:** Walden Woods Project archival quotation.

**Use and caveat:** The archive supplies the dated primary quotation.

### R37

**George Orwell. [Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/).** 1946.

**Passage:** Final discussion and six rules.

**Edition/source:** Orwell Foundation authorized text.

**Use and caveat:** Make words answer to meaning; the final rule overrides mechanical compliance.

## Systems, models, and intervention

### R38

**Warren Weaver. [Science and Complexity](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/warren-weaver-science-and-complexity-1948.pdf).** 1948.

**Passage:** American Scientist 36, pp. 536–544; Problems of Organized Complexity.

**Edition/source:** Authorized E:CO reprint, 2004.

**Use and caveat:** Structured interactions require more than isolated variables or simple averaging.

### R39

**Herbert A Simon. [The Architecture of Complexity](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/15-440/assets/READINGS/simon-architecture-of-complexity-1962.pdf).** 1962.

**Passage:** Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society 106, pp. 467–482; especially pp. 473–479.

**Edition/source:** Original paper scan, Carnegie Mellon.

**Use and caveat:** Nearly decomposable systems and economical descriptions.

### R40

**Edsger W Dijkstra. [On the Role of Scientific Thought](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD04xx/EWD447.html).** 1974.

**Passage:** EWD447.

**Edition/source:** University of Texas archive transcription.

**Use and caveat:** Separation of concerns as disciplined attention.

### R41

**David L Parnas. [On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules](https://www.cs.lafayette.edu/~gexia/cs301/resources/parnas.html).** 1972.

**Passage:** Communications of the ACM 15, pp. 1053–1058; The Criteria and Conclusion.

**Edition/source:** Primary paper transcription, Lafayette College.

**Use and caveat:** Choose module boundaries around design decisions and expose only necessary commitments.

### R42

**Frederick P Brooks Jr. [No Silver Bullet](https://worrydream.com/refs/Brooks_1986_-_No_Silver_Bullet.pdf).** 1986.

**Passage:** Essence and Accident in Software Engineering; Essential Difficulties.

**Edition/source:** Essay reproduction from The Mythical Man-Month anniversary edition.

**Use and caveat:** Conceptual complexity and implementation burdens; historical productivity predictions are not used as current forecasts.

### R43

**W Ross Ashby. [An Introduction to Cybernetics](https://ashby.info/Ashby-Introduction-to-Cybernetics.pdf).** 1956; second impression 1957.

**Passage:** Chapter 11, sections 11/5–11/9, printed pp. 204–208.

**Edition/source:** Author estate archive PDF.

**Use and caveat:** Requisite variety; specify disturbances, responses, and protected outcomes.

### R44

**Claude E Shannon. [Coding Theorems for a Discrete Source With a Fidelity Criterion](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/algorithm/information/1959-shannon.pdf).** 1959.

**Passage:** Opening discussion, printed pp. 325–327.

**Edition/source:** Original paper reproduction.

**Use and caveat:** Information requirements depend on the permitted distortion.

### R45

**Jorma Rissanen. [Modeling by Shortest Data Description](https://research.ibm.com/publications/modeling-by-shortest-data-description).** 1978.

**Passage:** Automatica 14, pp. 465–471; DOI 10.1016/0005-1098(78)90005-5.

**Edition/source:** IBM Research publication record and original abstract.

**Use and caveat:** The publication record establishes the original proposal; technical detail is supported by reference 46.

### R46

**Peter Grunwald. [A Tutorial Introduction to the Minimum Description Length Principle](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~pdg/ftp/mdlintro.pdf).** Author manuscript.

**Passage:** Sections 1.2–1.6, especially manuscript pp. 11–13.

**Edition/source:** CWI author manuscript.

**Use and caveat:** Introductory two-part MDL and qualifications about coding and refined methods.

### R47

**George E P Box. [Science and Statistics](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1976-box.pdf).** 1976.

**Passage:** Journal of the American Statistical Association 71, pp. 791–799; sections 2.1–2.5.

**Edition/source:** Original paper reproduction.

**Use and caveat:** Iterative modeling, parsimony, and consequential inadequacies.

### R48

**Donella H Meadows. [Leverage Points Places to Intervene in a System](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).** 1999.

**Passage:** Revised twelve-item list and concluding cautions.

**Edition/source:** Donella Meadows Project author archive.

**Use and caveat:** Structure, feedback, rules, goals, and the explicitly tentative ordering.

### R49

**Donella H Meadows. [Dancing With Systems](https://donellameadows.org/archives/dancing-with-systems/).** 2001.

**Passage:** Especially sections 1–3 and 8–11.

**Edition/source:** Donella Meadows Project author archive.

**Use and caveat:** Observe behavior, expose mental models, revise boundaries, and learn.

### R50

**Elinor Ostrom. [A Diagnostic Approach for Going Beyond Panaceas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2000497/).** 2007.

**Passage:** PNAS 104, pp. 15181–15187; DOI 10.1073/pnas.0702288104.

**Edition/source:** Original open-access paper, PMC.

**Use and caveat:** A nested diagnostic framework without a universal institutional remedy.

### R51

**James C Scott. [Seeing Like a State](https://djvu.online/file/W0O3DuxZ36wNM).** 1998.

**Passage:** Introduction; chapters 1 and 9; conclusion p. 345.

**Edition/source:** Original book text reproduction.

**Use and caveat:** Legibility, excluded local knowledge, and reversible interventions.

## Overview and additional primary reading

### R52

**Alan Baker. [Simplicity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/simplicity/).** Substantive revision 2022.

**Passage:** Sections 1–6.

**Edition/source:** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; scholarly secondary overview.

**Use and caveat:** Distinguishes theoretical elegance and ontological parsimony and reviews their justification.

### R53

**Henry David Thoreau. [Walden](https://henrythoreau.org/walden/chapter02-where_i_lived.html).** 1854.

**Passage:** Chapter II, Where I Lived and What I Lived For.

**Edition/source:** Primary text at Thoreau and Beyond.

**Use and caveat:** Simplifying affairs in relation to a considered purpose for living.
